## The model

`normalize_m` on `CoreProblemL` models `normalize` of `b709dd8` (keep-order variant) without the
`v = t` substitution. It follows the Rust functions one by one, with `QFBV_Lower`'s `lst` state (new
definitions and their widths, natural-number indices, `cr`, `push_m`) and `neg_t` for `cneg`:
`comm_op` (`commutative`), `norm_eq_m`, `remap_m`, `ref_of` (`ref_index(_, MAX)`, `None` for `MAX`),
`is_ones_m`, `not_of_m`, `mul_refs`, `factors_m`, `product_factors_m`, `rewrite_m`, `find_def_m`,
`mk_raw_m`, `mul_step` (the loop of `mk`), `mk_m`, `norm_loop` (the loop of `normalize`, over the
old definitions with the map `M`), `normalize_m`. `term_eq`/`same_bits` are `=`, `sort_usize` is
`List.sort`, `def_widths` is `cdef_widths`.

Two deviations, both in the theory's header text:
- The hash buckets are not modelled: `find_def_m` returns the first definition of the width that
  is `norm_eq_m` to the term. The contract of `find_def` (item 3) needs the invariant that no two
  new definitions of the same width are `norm_eq`, and that `norm_eq` terms have equal hashes.
- `factors_m` treats a product as a leaf unless both operand indices are below `k`. In a
  well-sorted problem they always are; the guard gives termination of the HOL function. The
  contract of `factors` must show the guard holds (from `cdefs_wt`).

## Theorems (all proved, no `sorry`, `thm_oracles normalize_correct` is empty)

`normalize_correct`: for `cproblem_wt q` and `15 * length (CoreProblemL_defs q) < 2 ^ 64`,
1. `CoreProblemL_widths (normalize_m q) = CoreProblemL_widths q` (so `cvar_widths` and
   `cis_assignment` are unchanged);
2. `cproblem_wt (normalize_m q)`;
3. `cis_model (normalize_m q) m ⟷ cis_model q m` for every `m`.

`normalize_defs_length`: under the same hypotheses, the new problem has at most
`15 * length (CoreProblemL_defs q)` definitions. (The hypothesis `15 * |defs| < 2^64` keeps the
`word_of_nat` of `cr` injective; it follows from `fb_hyps`/`lower_lens55`, `|defs| < 2^55`.)

The proof is the induction of the entry of #77 (on the old definitions, with `M`), as the
invariant `ninv`: `lok` of the new state, and for every old `j`, `M ! j` is a new definition with
the width (`menv`) and, under every in-range assignment, the value (`venv`) of `j`. Per step:
`remap_wt`/`remap_eval`, then `mk_ok`, built from `rewrite_sem` (the three rewrites; `x * ones`
uses `mul_ones` and `neg_t_sem` of `QFBV_Lower`), `mk_raw_ok` (sharing, `norm_eq_sem`),
`factors_ok`/`product_ok` (the product of the factors equals the product modulo `2^w`),
`mul_fold` (rebuilding the sorted product from the left) and `pv_sort`. A
helper `lok_nth` gives the width and value of any definition of a well-formed state.

## Deliverable 3: the bounds — the slack does not cover it; a change in `core/` is needed

What `solve` needs for `q' = normalize_m q` (q = `lower_problem_m p`) before `elim_facts`:
- `csize_bound q' ≤ urust_fuel` and `fuel_bound q' ≤ urust_fuel`: these are **not** a problem. The
  fuel is existential in `solve_correct` (`∃N. ∀fuel ≥ N`), and `fuel_top` (`proof/top`, not
  `spec/`) can add `size_bound` of the normalized problem (or 16 times `size_bound`: every new
  definition is a `Mul` of two references of a width of the input, or a `neg_t`/constant no
  heavier than twice the product it replaces).
- `fb_hyps q'` (`QFBV_ElimSpec`): every count `< 2^56`. This is the problem. `lower_fb_hyps` gets
  `fb_hyps q` from `sizes_ok p`, i.e. `4 * (1 + Σws + |ws| + |D| + |A| + 4·SUM + EC) < 2^56`,
  with `|defs q| ≤ 2·SUM` and `Σ ccount ≤ EC`. That leaves a factor of about 4 for the `ccount` sum
  and about 8 for the number of definitions — not 15. And `sizes_ok` itself has only a factor of
  about 1.9 of slack over `smt_in_range` (`tr_size_bound`: `4·(2^48 + 2^30 + 2^53)` ≈ `2^55.05`).
- The growth can really reach about 11–15 times: a product with up to 16 factors becomes up to 15
  new `Mul` definitions (each of `ccount` quadratic in the width), and sharing removes the old
  product tree only if it is shared. Example: `C` with 7 factors, shared; then for each `k`,
  `B_k = C * y_k` and `d_k = A * B_k` (`A` with 8 factors), with each `y_k` an early definition so
  that it sorts first. That gives 22 new products for every 2 old definitions. So `fb_hyps q'`
  cannot be derived from `sizes_ok` for the model as it is, and tightening the Rust `size_ok` by
  16 times is not possible either: `check_sat` must not answer `Unknown` inside `smt_in_range`, and
  `smt_in_range` (in `spec/`) leaves only a factor of about 1.9.

So no `csize_bound`/`fb_hyps` lemma is proved here. Options for item 3 (not made: `core/` must not
change in this issue):
1. **Budget in `normalize`** (recommended): flatten a product only while the number of extra
   definitions the flattening has added so far stays below the number of old definitions (or
   below the number of old `Mul` definitions, counted with their widths for `ccount`). Then
   `|defs q'| ≤ 2·|defs q|` and `Σ ccount q' ≤ 2·Σ ccount q` (each extra `Mul` of width `w` is
   charged to an old `Mul` of width `w`), and the factor 4 / 8 of slack in `lower_fb_hyps` is
   enough. The model gets one more counter in `norm_loop`. A benchmark run would tell whether the
   budget costs any of the 98/101.
2. Fall back to the unnormalized `q` when the normalized one is too large (a runtime check of
   `fb_hyps`-like counts on `q'`, as `size_ok` does). Simpler for the proof, but the check must be
   in the subset and proved against `ccount`/`tbound`.
3. Lower the cap from 16 to 4 or 5 factors: growth at most 3–4 times, which fits the factor of
   4 only for `ccount` and only just; not recommended.
