- **wienand distrib64** (unsat). Asserts `x*(y+z) ≠ x*y + x*z` over 129-bit zero-extended 64-bit
  inputs, with every subterm named by a variable (`var31 = var10 * var23`, `var23 = var8 + var14`, …).
  Lowered: 73 definitions; normalized: 49 (the substitution removes the named variables). It has
  three 129-bit `bvmul` and two `bvadd`, and the same after `elim`. The CNF has 50703 variables and
  185229 clauses (530k literals); encoding takes 0.03 s. All the time goes to the CDCL search: after
  35 s, 207k conflicts and 1.5M decisions; learned clauses average 68–238 literals. z3's SAT solver
  does not solve this CNF in 60 s either. This is the known hardness of multiplier equivalence for
  bit-level solvers. Only a word-level step closes it. With the products named as definitions,
  `x*(y+z)` can be rewritten to the existing `x*y + x*z`; the sharing then gives `Eq(r, r)`, which
  is 1.
- **bruttomesso ext_con_004_004_0064** (unsat). 64-bit `a`, `v1..v4`, four 4-bit dummies.
  Disequalities of nibbles of each `vi`, and a disjunction over `i` of equalities between 12-bit
  extracts of `a` and concatenations of `vi` slices and dummies. Lowered: 319 definitions (72
  extract, 32 concat, 48 eq, 32 and, 15 or, 16 not); normalized: 224; no arithmetic. The CNF is
  small: 1232 variables, 3135 clauses. Our CDCL had not finished after 127k conflicts (35 s). The
  conflict rate falls from 11k/s to 1.2k/s as the learned clauses grow to 130k, and propagation
  takes 94 % of the time. z3's SAT solver refutes **the same CNF in 314 conflicts (0.01 s)**. So this
  is a weakness of our search, not of the encoding. I tested which part matters by running our
  `dpll` on transformed copies of the CNF (a DIMACS reader on the scratch copy):
  - permuting the variables at random: solved in 0.01–0.02 s (578–706 conflicts);
  - reversing the variable order: solved in 0.05 s (2254 conflicts);
  - flipping polarities at random, shuffling the literals within clauses, or shuffling the clauses:
    not solved in 5 s.

  Other changes did not help: initial phase false, no restarts, dropping level-0 literals from
  learned clauses, or local clause minimization (learned clauses 34 → 9.5 literals). I also
  checked that propagation misses no unit (a full scan every 97 decisions found none) and that no
  learned clause is learned twice. **The cause is the decision heap's tie-break by the lower
  index.** `encode` numbers the input bits first, so among variables of equal activity the search
  decides the inputs first and keeps working on them: it enumerates the inputs of the circuits.
- **Sage2 bench_2404** (sat). 16 8-bit variables, 105 assertions. Lowered: 870 definitions (77

## The changes (uRust subset)

**Change 1 (`modes`).** `before` takes a flag `rev`: ties go to the lower index, or to the higher one
if `rev`. The eight heap functions pass it through. `dpll` keeps a second activity vector `act2`
(zeros) and `rev = false`. At every restart it swaps `act` and `act2`, flips `rev` and rebuilds the
heap (`heap_rebuild`). A rescale halves both vectors. Each order has its own activities: an order's
bumps are kept while the other order runs. No new definition, no change of the clauses.

**Change 2 (`distrib`).** In `mk`, after `rewrite`: if the term is a product `a * b` of two
definitions and `b` is a sum `x + y` of two definitions (`sum_of`), the two products `a * x` and
`a * y` are looked up with `lookup` (the bucket of `term_hash` and `find_def`, as `mk_raw` does,
so either operand order matches through `norm_eq`). If both are earlier definitions of width `w`,
the term becomes `Add(Ref p, Ref q)`. The same holds for `(x + y) * b`, giving `x*b + y*b`. The
operands keep the input's order (`a` stays on the left). If the lookup fails, nothing changes.
So the rewrite never adds a product, and it adds at most one definition: the sum, which
`mk_raw` may also find already present.

## Proof obligations

**Change 1.** Correctness of `dpll` does not depend on the order: the pure CDCL model of #56
(`QFBV_Cdcl.thy`, entailment and measure) allows any unassigned decision variable. The heap model
(`QFBV_Heap.thy`, #76, item 1 of #67) says that "the heap order is not needed for correctness and
is not proved". So the change extends that model:

- `before_m` gets the flag (`… ∨ (act ! u = act ! v ∧ (if rev then u > v else u < v))`), and
  `heap_up_m` … `heap_bumped_m` pass it. The lemmas on `hinv`, the element sets and the index
  bounds only case-split on `before_m`, so their proofs should go through with the extra argument.
- The `dpll` contract's invariant gains `length act2 = n`. `heap_rebuild` after the swap needs
  `hinv` and `length act = n`, which `heap_back` before it already gives.
- Termination is unchanged: the restarts and the swap stay under the existing cap (`restarts <
  2^20`), and the measure of #56 does not involve activities.

No new definition.

**Change 2.** It extends item 2 of #77's verification plan (the rewrites in `mk`, value preservation
under `ceval`) by one rewrite:

- Value: for definitions of width `w`, `(a·(x+y)) mod 2^w = ((a·x mod 2^w) + (a·y mod 2^w)) mod 2^w`
  (`distrib_left`, `mod_add_eq`, `mod_mult_right_eq`, as for `bits_nat_badd`/`bmul_m`), and the
  mirror for `(x+y)·b`. Then `ceval (Add (Ref p) (Ref q))` equals `ceval (Mul (Ref a) (Ref b))` once
  `p`, `q` are shown to have the values of `a·x`, `a·y`. That is the sharing lemma that `mk_raw`
  already needs: `find_def` returns `k` only if `widths ! k = w` and `norm_eq (defs ! k) t`, and
  `norm_eq` implies equal `ceval` (commutativity for the swapped case).
- Widths: `p`, `q` have width `w` by `find_def`'s width test. `x`, `y` have width `w` because `b` is
  an operand of a width-`w` product (`cwt`). So `cproblem_wt` is kept.
- Bound: at most **1** new definition per input definition (the sum; the products must exist).
  The result is a sum, so `product_factors` fails on it and the flattening (cap 16, at most 15
  new) does not also apply. The per-input-definition bound of #79 is still 16. `lookup` adds
  nothing. Its termination is `find_def`'s (`next[k] < k`).
- Contracts: `sum_of`, `lookup` (a wrapper of `find_def`) and `distribute` are non-recursive and
  straight-line over the existing contracts (`ref_index`, `op_code`, `term_hash`, `find_def`,
  `cbin`, `cref`).
